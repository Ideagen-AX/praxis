Nothing generic is defined. .cn-modal is the Create New modal and .filter-drawer is the filter drawer — both complete, both bound to one feature. Tier 1 on ROADMAP.md.

The ARIA Authoring Practices Guide lists Alert and Message Dialogs as a pattern separate from Dialog, and the distinction is the whole point of this page: a message dialog interrupts to ask one question with two answers. Voiding a permit, deleting an audit finding, discarding an unsaved incident — in a regulated application these are the moments that most need a consistent, unmistakable surface, and Praxis currently offers none.

Anatomy

  1. Scrim — dims and blocks the page. Three exist already (.cn-scrim, .drawer-scrim, .mazlan-scrim) and they should collapse into one.
  2. Panel — the dialog itself, centred, small.
  3. Title — a question or a statement of consequence, not a noun.
  4. Body — one short paragraph. What will happen and what cannot be undone.
  5. Footer — cancel and confirm. Order and emphasis are decisions, below.

Variants

VariantUseConfirm button
--confirmReversible. "Submit for approval?".tbtn--primary
--dangerIrreversible or destructive. "Void this permit?"A red confirm — and Praxis has no red button. .filter-drawer .btn--clear is the closest thing and it is scoped to the filter drawer
--alertOne acknowledgement, no choice. "Your session expired."A single dismiss

The open decision: whether the destructive confirm is a filled red button or a neutral button with red text. A filled red primary is the industry default, but Praxis's primary is a teal gradient with a lit edge (--px-primary-grad), and a red equivalent would be the second gradient recipe in the system.

States

  • Closed — hidden. Every Praxis overlay uses the [hidden] attribute rather than a class, and this should match.
  • Open — .is-open on the panel and scrim for the transition, following .filter-drawer and .px-navdrawer.
  • Confirm pending — the confirm button disabled with a spinner while the action is in flight. Depends on the loading component, also Tier 1.

Code

Belongs in praxis-controls.css with a script, for the same reason as the tooltip. Focus trapping, restore-on-close and Escape are not expressible in CSS.

The <dialog> element is worth considering seriously: it gives the focus trap, the top layer, the scrim via ::backdrop and Escape for free. The reason to hesitate is that Praxis's three existing overlays are all div-based with their own scrim elements, so adopting it here makes this component structurally unlike its neighbours.

Usage guidelines

Do

  • Name the consequence in the title. "Void permit PTW-2024-0031?", not "Are you sure?"
  • Label the confirm button with the verb. "Void permit", not "OK".
  • Say what cannot be undone, in the body, in one sentence.

Don't

  • Use it for anything reversible — that is a toast with an undo.
  • Put a form in it. A dialog with fields is a modal, and that is .cn-modal's territory.
  • Stack two. If confirming raises another question, the flow is wrong.

Accessibility

  • Focus must be trapped while open and restored on close. These are the two things that get missed and the two that make a dialog unusable by keyboard.
  • Escape closes and counts as cancel.
  • The scrim is aria-hidden and not focusable; clicking it cancels.
  • Initial focus on the safe action.
  • Forced colors: the panel separates from the scrim by shadow alone, which is dropped. Needs a border in that mode.
