Nothing exists. Praxis has no sign-off control, no attestation statement and no signed-record display. Tier 2 on ROADMAP.md, and first-class by decision 3 there.

This is the one component on the roadmap that exists because of the domain rather than because other design systems have it. In regulated environments an electronic signature is not a styled button: it has to capture who signed, when, and what the signature means — approval, review, authorship, responsibility — and it usually has to re-verify identity at the moment of signing.

The reference points are FDA 21 CFR Part 11 and EU GMP Annex 11. Neither is a design specification, and both constrain this component more than any style guide does. Nothing here should be treated as a compliance opinion — the point of writing it down is that the UI obligations are stable even where the legal ones vary by customer.

Anatomy

  1. Attestation statement — the sentence the user is agreeing to, shown in full. Not a link to terms, not a truncated summary.
  2. Meaning — which act this signature represents.
  3. Identity confirmation — re-entered credentials or a second factor.
  4. Sign control — deliberately not a default-focused primary.
  5. Signed record — the durable display afterwards: name, meaning, timestamp, and what was signed.

Variants

VariantUse
Single signatureAn inspector closing a finding
Co-signatureTwo roles must sign, often in order. The UI has to show whose turn it is
WitnessedA second person attests to the first signing. Present in nuclear and pharma operations
Signed record (read-only)The display form. Appears in the audit trail and on print

Open decision: whether Praxis ships the identity-confirmation step at all, or only the attestation and the signed record. Re-authentication is an application and identity-provider concern and getting it wrong is a security problem, not a styling one. The narrower scope — attestation, meaning, and signed-record display — is the safer thing for a design system to own.

States

  • Unsigned — the statement visible, the control enabled only once the record is valid.
  • Awaiting my signature versus awaiting someone else's. Different, and routinely conflated.
  • Signing — in flight. Must not be double-submittable.
  • Signed — immutable display.
  • Invalidated — the record changed after signing, so the signature no longer covers it. This is the state that matters most and the one most often missing; a stale signature presented as current is a compliance failure.
  • Failed — identity not confirmed. Must not clear the form.

Usage guidelines

Do

  • Show the full attestation statement.
  • Record what the signature means, explicitly.
  • Show whose signature is outstanding.
  • Mark a signature invalidated when the record changes underneath it.

Don't

  • Auto-focus the sign control.
  • Hide the statement behind a link.
  • Let one button mean approval on one screen and review on another.
  • Treat this as a styled submit. It is the most consequential control in the product.

Accessibility

  • The statement is associated with the control, so a screen-reader user hears what they are signing before they activate it.
  • No auto-focus on a consequential control.
  • State — awaiting, signed, invalidated — carried in text, not colour.
  • Errors must not clear the form, because re-authentication may have to be retried.
  • The signed record is readable text, not an image of a signature.
